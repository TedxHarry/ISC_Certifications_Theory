# ISC Certifications teaching specification

Version 1.0. October 29, 2026.
Applies to every learner lesson, reference aid and later revision in this course.

## Audience and result

Teach one beginner or fresher with basic workplace-account familiarity. They should be able to understand a lesson without remembering another lesson or a fictional story.

The learner needs a usable explanation: what the concept means, why it matters, which records and people participate, what information is used, how a decision or operation works, why the outcome follows and how to investigate a different result.

Theory develops independent explanation and reasoning with requirements and supplied evidence. Practical production operation also needs later hands-on experience and environment-specific permissions, configuration and procedures. Do not promise competence in every future feature or custom task.

## Voice and language

Write as a patient mentor sitting beside the learner. Use simple English, connected paragraphs and accurate terms. Use “you,” “let's” or “we” naturally when following a value or decision together.

Warmth comes from helping the learner make sense of the subject. Avoid greetings, forced slang, motivational filler, repeated reassurance and claims of personal production experience.

Prefer concrete verbs and explain unfamiliar nouns. A sentence can be grammatically simple and still be conceptually compressed. Expand the missing reasoning rather than replacing technical words with vague substitutes.

Do not introduce long lists of unfamiliar objects before explaining the first useful relationship. Introduce a term when its purpose becomes visible, explain its plain meaning, then show what it does in the present situation.

Do not use textbook-like definition blocks as a substitute for explanation, or technical-blog summaries as a substitute for the missing steps. Avoid inflated language, em dashes, canned conclusions and repeated negative comparisons.

## Independence without announcements

Make the lesson understandable on its own. Establish what exists, what changed or is being requested, what is expected and which supplied conditions apply.

Explain supporting ISC terms in context before depending on them. The terminology register is an author consistency aid; a definition link cannot supply necessary teaching.

Do not tell the reader that the lesson is independent, that terms will be introduced, how the course was designed or which template is being followed. Keep prerequisites, revision metadata and source-check dates outside the learner body.

Do not depend on another course lesson or an unwritten future lesson to explain the current mechanism.

## Direct teaching with small neutral examples

Teach the subject directly. Do not build lessons around a fictional company, a named employee, a biography or a continuing scenario.

A selected access item, field value, reviewer assignment, supplied report row or before/after result is useful when it reveals the mechanism. Include only identifiers needed for the reasoning.

For an example, establish what already exists, the relevant input/configuration, the operation or comparison and the supplied result. Distinguish it from an observed tenant outcome.

Define the meaning of example access: an item called reports-export should be explained as permission to export reports. Do not assume a name demonstrates its business purpose.

Preserve the conditions that make an outcome valid. For example, ordinary-account selection, eligible reviewer, appropriate engine, final decision and supported write capability may be decisive. State conditions where they matter, without burying the normal explanation in unrelated cautions.

A calculation, recorded decision or successful API response is not automatically the intended business outcome. Follow the relevant records and evidence.

## Explain the mechanism and its connections

For each substantial subject, cover the purpose, actors, records, inputs, configuration, trigger, decision or operation, outcome and relevant evidence. These are author checks, not mandatory headings or a fixed order.

Explain the transitions. If a review item is generated from collected access, say where that information came from. If a reviewer is chosen by a manager relationship, explain which relationship is used. If removal depends on a finalized decision, distinguish that condition from merely selecting Revoke.

Finish an understandable normal path before introducing variants when that helps. Then change a meaningful condition: stale source data, excluded identity, wrong owner, unsigned review, denied write or another assignment keeping access.

Where decisions branch, explain what is tested and why a branch is taken. Do not use “additional logic” to hide reasoning essential to the lesson.

Connect the UI to what it represents: a configuration instruction, generated snapshot, assignment, item decision, operation result or current account state. Documented backend behavior means supported processing and object relationships, not invented internal tables, queues or private services.

Explain asynchronous work without invented durations or universal retry promises. A requested operation, accepted request, completed work and verified target result can be different observations.

## Important distinctions to preserve

Keep these meanings consistent wherever relevant:
- Campaign definition/template, schedule, generated campaign and activation.
- Assigned certification, subject and individual review item.
- Person/identity, account, entitlement, access profile and role.
- Campaign owner, assigned reviewer, actual decision maker, source/access owner and remediation responsibility.
- Responsibility for work and permission to perform a platform action.
- Population selection, access refinement, campaign filtering and certifiable-access rules.
- Collected data, generated review data and live target state.
- Saved decision, signed-off/finalized review and administrator campaign completion.
- Removal requirement, fulfillment attempt, task/operation completion and verified access outcome.
- Revoking an assignment and removing all effective access.
- Stable concepts, engine-specific behavior, phased availability and announced future functionality.

Do not force every distinction into every lesson. Include the ones needed for its reasoning.

## Topic-led structure and length

There is no target for modules, lessons, words, headings, examples, diagrams, tables or questions. The current outline is a flexible coverage inventory.

Each lesson needs a distinct learning question and outcome. A term that needs two sentences does not automatically deserve a separate lesson. An overloaded lesson may need splitting. Remove genuine duplication while preserving supporting explanations that an independent reader needs.

Use prose for reasoning, a table for a useful comparison and a diagram for a relationship or branching flow. Do not reproduce a successful pilot's surface layout as a compulsory template.

Common task competence drives the core. The core includes necessary recognition of uncorrelated/non-human accounts, available decision-support context and basic API/status literacy. Deeper job-dependent routes cannot replace explanations the core needs.

## Diagrams and reference aids

Use a diagram only when it makes the relationship, branch, timing boundary or data path easier to understand. Explain its labels before relying on them and explain what its arrows mean.

Separate automatic processing from human verification. A settings hierarchy is not an execution sequence; a dependency map is not a promise that every action runs in that order.

Keep diagram sources editable and synchronized with the lesson. Plain text diagrams should normally fit a narrow reading area; choose the presentation to suit the actual diagram rather than impose a universal width or diagram count. Test visual presentation when publishing.

Place meaningful prose near every diagram so the relationships remain understandable without the visual.

Reference aids must be complete and understandable by themselves, with author-maintained revision/source-check information. Needed reasoning also appears inline in the lesson. Do not create empty aids for a future topic.

## Questions and evidence of learning

Use questions where they test understanding: explain a result, predict a changed input, select a useful next check, identify missing evidence or justify a decision.

Supply enough facts to reason. A question with insufficient evidence can legitimately ask the learner to identify what is missing; it cannot demand a fabricated definite result.

Worked answers explain why. Identify the correct record and the evidence that supports the conclusion. Avoid answer-only lists and questions that merely repeat a preceding definition.

Each complete learning unit needs an opportunity to demonstrate its reasoning and transfer it to a changed or unfamiliar case. Use a format appropriate to the topic; there is no fixed number of exercises or mandatory quiz section.

An independent learner assessment checks accurate terms, correct objects/inputs, causal reasoning, changed-condition prediction and evidence selection without coaching. Reading every lesson is not itself a pass.

Simulated author review and observed learner performance are separate. When no beginner participant has been tested, record actual learner validation as pending.

## Technical accuracy and current updates

Use primary SailPoint administration, reviewer and developer documentation, plus authoritative release information. Keep ISC separate from IdentityIQ and File Access Manager.

Use the proposed next-generation baseline where supported; identify a bounded legacy or conditional variant where needed. Verify availability, license, permissions, campaign type and engine behavior before making a claim.

Use the capability/update register. A source checked while scoping the curriculum is not verification of an unwritten lesson. Recheck claims during authoring and affected content before publication.

Where documentation conflicts, record the conflict and its affected claims. Seek authoritative clarification or an explicitly permitted controlled observation; otherwise teach a verified narrower scope without pretending the uncertainty is resolved.

Do not fabricate a tenant test, support answer, result, timing, screenshot or learner experience. Do not add exact API payloads, syntax or report fields until the current contract has been verified.

## Source references and learner presentation

Keep external documentation URLs, citation markers, source lists, verification dates, revision notes and review metadata out of learner lesson bodies. Store claim-to-source references in the per-lesson author record.

Planning documents and author references may contain links. Removing sources from the learner does not reduce the requirement to verify them.

The later site may show press-and-hold term definitions: press and hold to open, release to close, with touch and keyboard equivalents. Hover alone should not trigger it. Essential explanations remain in the prose. This is a future presentation requirement, not an implemented interaction.

## Complete-lesson review

Before calling a lesson author-reviewed:
1. Reconcile its intended coverage against the actual saved prose and relevant operational tasks.
2. Verify technical claims and example conditions against primary sources.
3. Read the entire saved lesson as an unfamiliar beginner, including its middle, ending and worked answers.
4. Resolve unexplained terms, skipped transitions, ambiguous actors, unsupported outcomes and unnecessary repetition.
5. Check all values, identifiers, branches, tables, diagrams and worked reasoning agree.
6. Test applicable file links and diagram/source correspondence; inspect publication rendering when available.
7. Record what was actually checked, unresolved limitations, learner-validation status and publication status separately.

The per-lesson record contains the evidence. Do not add review commentary to the learner.

## Writing and publication sequence

Produce one complete learner lesson at a time, with the assets and author record needed for that lesson. Update the course register only after the saved-file checks. Do not create unwritten-lesson stubs.

When a later instruction authorizes repository creation or pushing, use the intended separate destination ISC_Certifications_Theory. Current work remains local. Check the actual publishing scope and existing authorization; do not invent an additional confirmation requirement.

Keep independent learner validation and publication review truthful. They are distinct recorded activities, not an automatic user-approval requirement.

## Confirmed finding A01: a manual link can survive a new match

In005, “Changing a matching rule does not prove existing accounts were rechecked” explained optimized versus unoptimized processing, but did not tell the learner that an explicitly assigned identity link has different persistence.

A beginner could consequently expect a correctly configured unoptimized run to move every wrong association. Official correlation guidance explicitly says manual correlation persists despite attribute changes and is not removed by an unoptimized aggregation.

[Local CRT-005 revision1.2](../lessons/CRT-005_From_Application_Access_to_Reviewable_Information.md) now explains manual correlation where it becomes relevant, follows report-014's retained wrong link through a changed case, and points to authorized correction of the association followed by actual-link verification. It distinguishes that association from deletion of the application account and from deciding export need.

[The new revision record](revisions/CRT-005_Revision_1_2_Record.md) preserves the old records and documents the limited correction. Existing diagram and main examples remain intact. This repair stays local; the published005 remains1.1.

## Language and continuity decisions

Keep local supporting meanings. Repeated explanations of account, identity and review are useful for a reader opening a lesson directly. The first six lessons use them for different jobs: purpose, containment, whole flow, comparison, data connections and decision scope.

Do not shorten005 by deleting the schema-to-membership-to-catalog comparison, or004 by deleting the lifecycle input/settings/operation transitions. Those are the steps a novice needs to understand why the result follows. The useful repair is a missing distinction, not a shorter word count.

Do not turn the role/profile replacement discussion into a hands-on sequence until the actual shared-access behavior is established. Retained-access requirements and target outcomes remain explicit.

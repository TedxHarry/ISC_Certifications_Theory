## First complete-lesson brief

### Learner question and result

How does rejecting an inappropriate part of a role lead to a correction, and what would prove that the intended change happened?

A learner should explain the selected role, reviewed contents, reason for correction, responsible task, changed definition, required processing and exact account results. They should distinguish manually correcting a role from manually changing every application account.

### Useful opening

A role intended for viewing reports includes an export profile. Its reviewer rejects that profile. Later, someone closes the correction task. What needs to be true before we say the problem is fixed?

Introduce the role as a shared access definition when it first becomes necessary. Use short local account/profile/entitlement meanings. Do not begin with a glossary, engine history or a recap of the whole course.

### Supplied normal conditions

- An enabled, standard, non-dynamic role named Report Reader is eligible for the supported legacy composition route.
- Its approved purpose is viewing reports inside Reporting Production, without downloading report copies.
- Report Reading contains REPORT.VIEW, permission to view reports. Report Export contains REPORT.EXPORT, permission to export them. The role currently includes both profiles.
- Two identities hold the role. Their correctly linked production accounts currently hold both permissions, and ISC's collected account information is current. Both need viewing. No other assignment requires export in the normal case.
- The selected role owner understands the approved purpose, is permitted to review, and is also a role administrator explicitly selected for the correction work. Do not infer their authority merely from ownership.
- The generated work actually contains the intended role and profiles. The example requires rejection comments. The reviewer records the justified decisions and completes the required confirmation.
- When following the correction, an actual task exists for the selected permitted administrator. Its contents and recipient are supplied observations for this example, not an invented universal task schema.
- Supported role-removal propagation is enabled; the role remains enabled. The source supports and is authorized for the required account changes. Later processing and target results are supplied explicitly where used, without a fixed waiting time.

These are illustrative conditions, not instructions to alter a tenant or proof of an observed execution.

### Connected explanation to write

Begin with the unwanted export inside the shared role. Briefly distinguish the composition question from one person's need, then follow:

1. Select the eligible role and validate the actual generated contents and responsibilities. Only explain setup choices that affect this example.
2. Inspect what the two profiles allow and compare that scope with the role's approved purpose. Keep viewing; identify why export does not belong here.
3. Write a precise correction reason: remove Report Export from Report Reader while retaining Report Reading. Do not delete or redefine a shared profile merely because one role should stop including it.
4. Connect the finalized review with the actual assigned task. Read what must change, who can change it and what must remain.
5. The authorized administrator removes the profile association from this role and verifies its saved contents. That is a manual role-definition change.
6. Explain the separate effect on existing members: check propagation conditions, follow processing and any resulting provisioning, then inspect the affected account results. Automatic delivery after a human definition correction is not a contradiction.
7. Establish both parts of success: corrected role contents and the required member-account outcome, including retained viewing. Task closure alone does not supply both results.

The list is an author trace, not compulsory learner headings. Let the explanation develop naturally. A diagram may connect reviewed contents, manual definition correction and subsequent account work if it reduces confusion; no diagram/table/exercise quota.

### Meaningful variations

**Propagation disabled:** the definition is corrected, but that edit alone does not remove the old entitlement through role-content propagation. Identify the remaining account requirement and authorized follow-up. Do not suggest casually enabling a tenant-wide feature.

**Another justified role still supplies export:** the question concerns what Report Reader should contain. Removing export from that role does not establish that every affected person must lose all export access. Inspect the other assignment and its authorization.

**One holder's work changes instead:** consider a different role whose approved purpose legitimately includes export. One holder leaves that work while the others still need it. Ask whether this establishes a defect in the shared definition or a need to change that person's assignment. Make the changed purpose and affected population explicit before proposing a shared change.

Use the variations needed to explain the mechanism; combine or omit repetitive worked cases. Do not add a second full project walkthrough.

## Resume without losing the task

Read the course register for the actual next lesson and status. Check its coverage, routine tasks, term meanings, engine/capability records and open questions.

The outline is a coverage plan, not a list of finished articles. Adapt lesson boundaries when the content needs it, while preserving every committed concept and operational task. Record a move or merge rather than silently dropping coverage.

For the next lesson, complete a short author brief: learner question, intended outcome, scope, supporting terms, starting conditions, useful normal mechanism, meaningful variations, evidence and assessment purpose. Do not impose that structure on the learner prose.

## Before drafting

1. Identify the lesson's distinct job and the topics fully owned elsewhere.
2. Verify relevant primary-source claims, engine/availability and permissions. Update the capability register where facts changed.
3. Select only the neutral inputs and identifiers needed. Record example conditions and any supplied outcome.
4. Choose an explanation order that makes sense to a beginner. A diagram or code fragment needs explanation before it carries new reasoning.
5. Decide what an unfamiliar changed case should reveal about understanding.

A future unresolved claim is not a reason to stop an unrelated lesson. For the affected claim, establish the supported scope or leave that claim out until resolved; do not invent a universal behavior.

## Draft and finish one learning unit

Write the whole explanation with its necessary supporting meanings. Complete the normal mechanism and relevant changed conditions before polishing headings or shortening wording.

Use lessons/CRT-001_Why_Review_Previously_Approved_Access.md for the first learner file. Other filenames are assigned when their lessons are authored. No file should exist only to announce an unwritten lesson.

Save editable diagrams in assets/ using the lesson ID and descriptive name. Create a reference aid when the lesson actually develops it. A learner diagram should explain a useful relationship, not decorate a page.

Create author/revisions/CRT-001_Revision_Record.md with the first complete learner. Subsequent author records use their actual lesson IDs. A record must identify the revision and include:
- Scope and source of authorization.
- Coverage-to-saved-section reconciliation, including relevant task-map rows.
- Claim-to-primary-source references, verification date and engine/availability conditions.
- Neutral inputs, assumptions, supplied outcomes and diagram consistency.

- Full saved-file beginner author read-through, refinements and actual checks.

Those are evidence fields, not compulsory learner headings or identical author-document layouts.

## Saved-file review

Read the entire saved lesson in manageable contiguous portions. Do not stop at a good opening or use keyword counts as proof of understandable teaching.

For each important transition ask: would the reader know which record changed, who did the work, which input or rule was used, why this result follows and which result remains unestablished?

Inspect and reread edited passages. Reconcile every example, diagram arrow and worked answer. Check factual qualifications without drowning the normal explanation in unrelated caveats.

Record only checks actually performed. Link/file checks and source verification do not establish visual rendering or a learner's understanding.

Update the course register, coverage status and any newly authored term/capability references after completing the checks. Name the next complete lesson. Preserve previous records rather than rewording old checks as though they happened again.

## First lesson brief: CRT-001

Working title: Why review access that somebody already approved?
Permanent learner file on authoring: lessons/CRT-001_Why_Review_Previously_Approved_Access.md.
Current status: authored revision 1.0 on October 29, 2026. [Learner](../lessons/CRT-001_Why_Review_Previously_Approved_Access.md) and [author record](revisions/CRT-001_Revision_Record.md). This brief records the scope used for the first lesson.

### Learner question and intended result

The learner should explain why an earlier valid access approval does not settle today's business need, what a certification review decides and why the decision needs a verified outcome.

A successful explanation connects changed responsibilities, existing access, current evidence, an appropriate reviewer, an access decision and applicable follow-up. It distinguishes historical approval, present review and actual access removal.

### Starting situation and useful neutral detail

Use an existing account with permission to export reports. The permission was previously approved for a task. The person's responsibilities later changed, and the account still has the permission.

If using a technical item label, explain its business meaning. No employee name, company biography, mailbox or arbitrary object ID is needed.

For the simple example, supply correct identity/account association, current represented access and an individually reviewable entitlement that is not encapsulated in a role/access profile or required by a lifecycle state. State only the conditions needed to follow this first lesson; detailed certifiability belongs to CRT-006/007.

The reviewer's knowledge of current need is supplied. Do not infer loss of need merely because a job title changed.

### Explanation to develop

Establish the practical problem before naming certification. Explain access, account, identity and entitlement in context. Introduce access certification as an assigned review of whether represented access remains appropriate.

Distinguish a past access-request approval from a current access review. A campaign briefly means the organized set of assigned reviews; detailed campaign objects belong to CRT-002.

Trace the normal business reasoning: what the access permits, why it was needed, what has changed, who has enough relevant knowledge, what evidence supports keeping/removing it and how the required outcome would be checked.

For removal, explain that a review decision and application access are different records/results. Introduce remediation as carrying out the removal requirement. Do not teach exact sign-off/completion timing, retry behavior or ambiguous engine status as though verified for every engine.

Approval means the reviewer accepts continued access under the reviewed conditions. It is not a request to create unrelated missing access. Do not imply that a review freezes future business change.

### Useful reasoning checks

Choose the checks that reveal the mechanism:
- Earlier approval is valid and current work still requires the access.
- Responsibilities changed but evidence of current need is missing.
- Revoke is recorded but the target still holds access.
- An identity view reflects older collection than the source observation.

The learner should justify an answer or identify missing evidence, not choose removal solely to make a review look strict. No fixed case count is required.

### Scope boundary

Complete purpose, present-need reasoning and outcome distinction. Briefly introduce needed terms, but defer detailed campaign routing, filters, deadline policies, API contracts, reporting fields and removal mechanics to their primary topics.

### Author source leads and unresolved claims

Use the official overview, Understanding Certifications and relevant reviewer guidance from the source map. Freshly check the actual claims while writing. Scoping checks already performed do not establish a finished learner.

The capability register records conflicting unsigned-decision and remediation-phase descriptions. The first lesson can teach the verified purpose and separate outcome without resolving those advanced exact-state claims.

### Acceptance of this lesson as author-reviewed

An unfamiliar reader can explain why review is needed, what is being reviewed, which knowledge supports a decision, how review differs from original approval and why the required target outcome needs evidence.

The saved lesson contains complete connected prose, useful reasoning/transfer, consistent supplied conditions and no biography, prerequisite announcement, source URLs or invented tenant results. The author record is complete; actual learner validation is separately pending unless it has really occurred.
